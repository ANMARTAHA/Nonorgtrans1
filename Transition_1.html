01 / Overview

The essentials, condensed

Every rule, number, and definition you actually need — no lecture-style padding.

Definition d-Block metals

  • Elements in groups 3–12 of the periodic table.
  • Also called transition metals.

IUPAC Transition metal rule

  • An element with an incomplete d subshell, or one that forms a cation with an incomplete d subshell.
  • Zn, Cd, Hg (group 12) are excluded — their d subshell is always full.
  • "Heavier d-block metals" = 2nd & 3rd row (4d, 5d series).

Group 10 Family at a glance

  • Nonradioactive: Ni, Pd, Pt. Radioactive: Ds.
  • Pd & Pt resist corrosion — attacked by O₂, F₂, Cl₂ only at very high temperature.
  • Pd dissolves in hot oxidizing acids.
  • Pt dissolves only in aqua regia (1:3 HNO₃:HCl).

Config Electron configurations

  • Ni: [Ar] 4s² 3d⁸
  • Pd: [Kr] 4d¹⁰
  • Pt: [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d⁹ 6s¹
  • Most stable oxidation state for all three: +II.
  • Pt(II) is d⁸ → dominant geometry is square planar.

Uses Catalytic roles

  • Pd → hydrogenation catalyst; H₂/D₂/T₂ separation & purification; Wacker process (ethylene → acetaldehyde).
  • Pt → oxidation catalyst in HNO₃ production.

Drug Cisplatin — core facts

  • cis-diamminedichloroplatinum(II) — neutral, inorganic, yellow powder.
  • First synthesized in 1844.
  • 2 NH₃ (nonleaving) + 2 Cl (labile, leaving).
  • Binds DNA → cross-linking → apoptosis.
  • Treats: ovarian, testicular, uterine, bladder, head & neck cancers.
  • Made from [PtCl₄]²⁻, stepwise addition of NH₃ (cis-directed).

MOA Cisplatin mode of action

  • Given IV. Extracellular [Cl⁻] ≈ 100 mM keeps it stable in blood.
  • Intracellular [Cl⁻] = 4 mM triggers hydrolysis inside the cell.
  • Forms electrophilic mono-/diaqua species.
  • Uptake: passive diffusion + active transport (copper & organic cation transporters).
  • Binds DNA mainly at N7-guanine; minor sites: N7/N1-adenine, N3-cytosine.
  • Mitochondrial DNA binding is less important for activity.

Risk Resistance & toxicity

  • Narrow therapeutic window — dose can't simply be raised.
  • Resistance: ↓ drug entry, ↑ efflux, ↑ DNA repair, removal of Pt complex.
  • Toxicity: nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, peripheral neuropathy.

2nd gen Carboplatin

  • cis-diamine(1,1-cyclobutanedicarboxylato)Pt(II) — Paraplatin®, FDA 1989.
  • Cl ligands replaced by a bidentate chelate (cbdca).
  • Less nephro/ototoxic (lower reactivity) but more myelosuppressive.
  • Dose 3–6× cisplatin; can be given outpatient.
  • Prodrug — chelate ring-opens after hydrolysis to react with DNA.
  • Uses: head & neck, ovarian, small-cell lung, testicular cancer.

3rd gen Oxaliplatin

  • cis-[oxalato]trans-1,2-diaminocyclohexane Pt(II) — Eloxatin®.
  • DACH ligand (nonleaving) + oxalato (leaving group).
  • Bulky DACH hydrolysis product blocks mismatch repair → excellent vs. colon/rectal cancer.
  • Combined with 5-FU/leucovorin for metastatic colorectal cancer.
  • Dose-limiting toxicity: nerve damage, possibly irreversible.
  • IV infusion over 2–6 h.

Pipeline Other platinum candidates

  • Nedaplatin — cis-diammineglycolato-Pt(II); Japan; head&neck/testicular/ovarian/lung/cervical; dose-limiting myelosuppression.
  • Lobaplatin — NSCLC & breast cancer. Heptaplatin — gastric cancer. Both: myelosuppression + mild hepatotoxicity, not EU/FDA approved.
  • Satraplatin — Pt(IV), oral, hydrophobic, no cross-resistance with cisplatin; phase III (+prednisone) for hormone-refractory prostate cancer, but no FDA fast-track (insufficient survival gain).
